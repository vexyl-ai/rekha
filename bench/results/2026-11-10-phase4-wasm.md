# Phase 4 baseline: WebAssembly latency, 2026-11-10

Branch `phase4-online`. The roadmap's Phase 4 exit test is "WASM latency measured; API survives fuzzed uploads". This file is the first half; the second is `FuzzTraceHandler` in `cmd/rekha-serve` (30 s, 2.4 million executions, no crash, recorded in the Task 4 commit).

## Environment

- MacBook, Apple Silicon, 10 cores. Go go1.26.4, Node v20.19.6, `wasm_exec.js` from the same Go.
- Native: `REKHA_LATENCY=1 REKHA_LOGO=$PWD/Logo.jpg go test -run TestReport -v ./internal/latency` at GOMAXPROCS 10 and 1.
- wasm: the same table from `cmd/rekha-wasm` `TestWasmLatency` under `go_js_wasm_exec` (Node). The wasm reads the corpus through Node's `fs` and decodes with Go's PNG/JPEG decoders inside the test, so the numbers below are the tracer only, as in the native table; the browser decodes with `createImageBitmap` instead.
- Image decoding is excluded from every row; a row is Segment + Finish for one preset.

## Reading it

- **Bar (author's, spec Task 5): wasm within 5× of one-core native.** Pass on the median (4.5×) and on 41 of 51 rows; fail on the strict every-row reading. Recorded as is.
- 256×256 images: every preset under 0.5 s in wasm except refine on poster-discs (1.2 s) and the 2-px checkerboard (6.6 s). Live sliders are fine at this size.
- The 3.4 MP logo in the browser: `bw` 0.7 s, `poster` 5.9 s, `poster --refine` 54 s. The demo's default 4 MP cap admits it; refine is a wait, not a slider. The page should run `poster` live and offer refine as a final step (not implemented; the demo exposes the checkbox).
- Before this branch, colour segmentation computed every cell of the 6-bit colour cube eagerly (`quant.NewLookup`), about 130 ms natively and 3.4 s in wasm per image regardless of size. Cells are now computed on first use and sRGB→linear is tabulated; output is byte-identical (every golden unchanged). This is the one tracing-package change in Phase 4.
- Memory: 337 MB peak for a 4 MP image under Node. Go's wasm heap does not shrink, so a page that traces several large images keeps the largest footprint; the demo's pixel cap is the control.

## Not measured

- Browser timings (Chrome's wasm JIT versus Node's are the same V8; a few spot checks in Chrome matched within 10 %: rounded-rect bw finish 108 ms, poster-discs poster segment 92 ms after the lookup change).
- Safari and Firefox.
- Multi-threaded wasm: not available to Go.
