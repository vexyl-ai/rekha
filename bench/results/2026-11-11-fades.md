# Fades in layered regions, and rescued seam pixels (2026-11-11)

The red tail of the Firefox mark ended in a wavy seam against the body
where the source has none: the dark band runs on into the body's lower
rim. The tail's ramp painted the tail; the body's layered model (a base
ramp under two translucent radial layers, fitted to 1.1 million pixels)
missed the rim darkening under the tip by 10–15 counts, so the seam was
the body's misfit meeting the tail's fit.

Two general changes. A layer's opacity may now rise along its parameter
instead of falling (a vignette, a body darkening towards its rim), and
the parameter may be the projection on a direction instead of the
distance from a centre (a shadow on one side), emitted as a
`<linearGradient>` with `stop-opacity`. And before the seam votes, a
seam pixel its own model misfits beyond tolerance moves to the
neighbouring label whose model paints it, the front following through
pixels painted wrong already and stopping where the old model comes
within tolerance. On the mark the tail runs some 5 000 pixels further
down the rim, where its ramp still fits, and the notch at its tip goes.

Real images (photo preset, `--gradients`; before = 0dadc04):

| Firefox mark | 35 → 33 | 36.33 → 36.34 dB | 33.9 → 32.7 kB | 4.2 → 4.5 s |

| Pokéball | 5 | 35.52 dB | same | same |
| green mark | 9 | 36.99 dB | same | same |

Under the tail's tip (the 220×120 box below it) the mean error against
the source falls from 6.7 to 3.2 counts. The seam is a smooth, faint
edge rather than a wavy one; it is not gone, because the body's model
still misfits its whole lower rim band by more than the tolerance. A
third overlay per region lifts the mark to 37.1 dB but costs 42 kB and
leaves the tip no better, so the limit stays at two. A continuation
fill (the tail painted with the body's gradients plus one fade from
the seam) was built and dropped: it never fires on a real image, since
where the host misfits the seam the fade cannot start at zero, and
where it fits the seam is within tolerance already.

Every golden is byte-identical.
