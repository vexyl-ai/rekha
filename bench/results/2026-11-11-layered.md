# Layered gradients (2026-11-11)

`--gradients` now paints a region shaded more than one way the way an
illustrator does: a base ramp along the region's direction, then up to
two translucent radial layers over it, each a flat colour whose opacity
falls off with the distance from its own centre. The fit alternates
least squares between the layers and the base on a sample of the
region's interior pixels, with the centres seeded from the residual and
refined by coordinate descent; it is tried only where both the linear
and the radial model fail, and kept only when it reproduces every
interior pixel within the same tolerance. Each layer is one more
`<path>` of the region's shape filled with a `<radialGradient>` whose
stops fade by `stop-opacity`.

Synthetic: a disc shaded by a ramp along x under a highlight inside it
and a rim light centred outside it traces to one region in three paths
(base and two layers) with RMS 0.012 against tol 0.03; the plain ramp
of the gradient fixtures gets no layer.

The Firefox globe, banded since the first gradient pass (its best
single-field model left RMS 0.085 over its pixels against the 0.03
tolerance), is one smooth region now: over the globe's pixels alone the
layered fit leaves RMS 0.012, with a violet layer on its upper right
and a blue one at its lower left, which is how the original is drawn.
The body's orange sweep joins across the band that split it. The gain
in bytes on the large mark is the band paths that the layered regions
replace; the small copy spends a little more on gradient definitions
than it saves in paths.

Cost: a fit is about half a second on its fixed-size sample whatever
the region's size, once per joined group that no single field
reproduces: the mark goes from 3.1 to 3.8 s, the small copy from 0.8 to
2.1 s, the bird from 11.9 to 10.2 s (fewer dissolves to replay).

Every golden is byte-identical; the corpus is unchanged.
