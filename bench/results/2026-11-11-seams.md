# Seams between gradient regions (2026-11-11)

Where two gradient regions meet across a soft boundary (neighbouring
pixels within four counts of each other) the exact line between them is
quantiser noise: both models paint the seam pixels within tolerance,
and the label boundary wanders in a staircase of two-to-four-pixel
steps. Filled flat, the steps hid inside the bands' own colour steps;
filled with smooth gradients they showed as ragged seams across the
Firefox mark's body.

Re-assigning each seam pixel to whichever model predicts it better was
tried first: the two models tie across a wide zone, so the front ran
some 20 000 pixels into the body and left a dithered fringe where it
stopped. The fix is geometric instead. After `Merge` each seam pixel
takes the label that holds the majority of the 11×11 window round it,
when that label's model paints the pixel within tolerance; a pixel with
fewer than two neighbours of its own label (a strand or an island,
which would become a path of its own) goes to the label most of its
neighbours carry whatever the fit. Crisp edges are untouched because
the other side's model misfits there.

Real images (photo preset, `--gradients`; before = 20d1380):

| image | paths | PSNR | bytes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firefox mark | 35 | 36.33 → 36.38 dB | 43.0 → 36.0 kB |
| letter G | 16 | 36.97 dB | 13.6 kB (same) |
| Pokéball | 5 | 35.52 dB | same |
| green mark | 9 | 36.99 dB | same |

The body's two long seams (orange band against the lower body, the red
tail against the pink base) are now smooth curves where they were
staircases; the tail's tip keeps a short whisker. The bytes fall
because each seam is one gentle curve instead of a run of steps.

Every golden is byte-identical.
