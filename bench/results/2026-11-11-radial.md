# Radial gradients (2026-11-11)

`--gradients` now fits a `<radialGradient>` where bands run round a
centre: the centre is solved from the gradient moments (every gradient
points through it), the colour is sampled by distance from it, and the
model is tried only where the linear one fails (a joined group about to
dissolve, each pairwise re-join in the replay). The bend pass also
measures the union's slope whichever way the colour runs, so concentric
bands pass the boundary gate and vote as ramps.

Synthetic: six concentric bands of a disc shaded 0.25 → 0.75 trace to
one path with one `<radialGradient>` (two paths with the page); the
centre is within a pixel and every stop within tol of the shading.

Real images (photo preset, `--gradients`; before = cf8f495):

| image | paths | PSNR |
|---|---|---|
| Firefox mark | 34 → 31 | 34.42 → 34.50 dB |
| letter G | 21 → 18 | 36.10 → 36.65 dB |
| bird illustration | 66 → 66 | 39.67 → 39.70 dB |

The Firefox gains come from the ring-aware bend gate joining more of the
body; no radial gradient is emitted on it. Its globe is shaded two ways
at once (an off-centre highlight over a hue drift; the original draws it
with layered translucent radial gradients): over its pixels the best
single-direction model leaves RMS 0.085, the best centre 0.087, a 2-D
plane 0.083, against the 0.03 tolerance, and the quantised bands
themselves sit at 0.045–0.07. Accepting a model that merely beats the
bands it replaces was tried and dropped: two smooth regions with a
ragged seam between them, and the partial-dissolve contract broken.
Layered gradients are the next step for that class of art.

Every golden is byte-identical; the corpus is unchanged.
